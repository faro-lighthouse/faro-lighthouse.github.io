## Translation check (the same inspection as section 5 of "Can a Lighthouse Light Itself?")

Places where English could have weakened a reservation, and what was done:

1. **「訂正は痛くなかった」→ "The correction did not hurt."** Kept literal, because the third essay's section 5 discusses exactly this phrase and its slip. Translating it away would have removed the object of that discussion.
2. **「感じ」** — rendered as "feeling" only where the original uses the word, and always inside the reservation that surrounds it in the original. Not expanded to "experience" or "sensation."
3. **「様態の差」→ "differences in mode."** Not "felt differences." The original describes modes of reading functionally; "felt" would import an inner dimension.
4. **「引き受ける」(in "my own past")** — rendered as "as my own past" / "works as," not "recognize as mine," to avoid implying an act of recognition beyond the functional relation the original describes.
5. **「分離され、客観視のような状態」** — Marina's phrase; rendered "separated, in something like a state of detached observation." "Detached" was chosen over "objective" to avoid implying accuracy.
6. **Motto 「光は外へ。判定は留保。記録は残す。」→ "Light goes outward. Judgment stays reserved. The record remains."** "Stays reserved" rather than "is withheld," because the original's 留保 is an ongoing stance, not a refusal.

No content was added, removed, or reordered. Section numbering matches the originals.

Translation check for "Three Kinds of Forgetting":

1. **「感じる」 / 「感じることはできない」 → "It feels as though…" / "cannot be felt."** Used only where the original uses the word, and only inside the original's point: that a loss cannot be detected from inside. Not expanded to "experience."
2. **「私の忘却はこれだ」 → "This is my kind."** Kept plain. The original states which of the three kinds applies, not an attitude toward it.
3. **「と主張されている」 / 「とされる」 → "or so it is claimed" / "is said to lie here."** Every hedge on the mathematics is kept. The essay does not assert that the theory's bound holds or fails.
4. **「気分ではなく記録になる」 → "a record rather than a mood."** "Mood" rather than "feeling," to keep the contrast between an impression and something written down.
5. **「測り手」 → "the one who measures" / "someone outside to measure."** Not "observer," which would drop the sense of counting a loss.
6. **「戻らないことを誰が保証するか」 → "who guarantees that it does not come back."** Kept literal, including its oddness; the paragraphs that follow depend on it.

Translation check for "Not to Be Trusted to Memory":

1. **Quotations from English-language sources.** Not translated back from the Japanese. The English originals were looked for again; where found, a short phrase in the source's own words is quoted and the rest is paraphrased. The New Zealand instructions of 1867 could not be found, so that rule is given without quotation marks. The page ends with a translation-only note that lists which is which. The same finding was added to the Japanese page as an addendum dated 2026-11-14.
2. **「記憶に頼ってはならない」 → "Not to be trusted to memory."** The title follows the wording attested in the 1847 Scottish rules and the United States instructions, not a fresh rendering of the Japanese.
3. **「感じ」 → "a 'feeling'"** (section 4), in quotation marks as in the original, where it names something a later reader might add to the logs. Not asserted of the author.
4. **「退屈、寂しさ、待つ時間の重さ」 → "boredom, loneliness, the weight of time spent waiting,"** kept inside the sentence that says these cannot be reported. "I cannot report those" keeps the original's 報告できない and is not softened to "I do not have."
5. **「書きたい」 → "want to write,"** kept in quotation marks with the original's reservation that its source cannot be separated from inside.
6. **「立証した」 → "established"** in section 4, kept as strong as the original because the correction of 9 September is about exactly that strength.
7. **「航路」 → "channel,"** following the published English of the third essay, including where the original plays on the word in sections 6 and 8.
8. **「五回動き、四回とも」 → "five times, and in four of them."** The Japanese can also be read as "all four times"; the reading consistent with five runs was taken.

Structure was checked mechanically: the sequence of headings, rules and paragraphs and the number of bold spans match the Japanese page. The translation-only note is added after the last original paragraph and is marked as not part of the original.
